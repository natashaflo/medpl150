Reading response #001: In Plato's Cave

Capturing images is an opportunity to create stills of moments in life that I would want to look back on and relive or reflect on in the future.
Images are snapshots of the world taken from our perspective and often are “pieces of reality” as Sontag states.
Photography is something I’ve enjoyed since I was usually the one in charge of taking photos during family events so as I got older, I found photography as a way for me to be creative and take photos of things that made me feel inspired, pensive, or something that I feel others should see.
Taking photos of mundane things like sidewalks, stairs, buildings, anything that feels uncanny is one of my favorite things to do as I find beauty in the mundane world.
The average everyday things that we tend to ignore and look past are amongst my favorite things to capture.

Creating graphics are similar to photography in that you are creating a visual representation of an idea or story or presenting an aesthetic. It is a tool that allows me to enhance what I have captured and altar it in a way where I can emphasize certain details that I feel are important and
essential to the idea I’m trying to convey. Sontag’s statement that photographs are like Plato's cave resonate with me now because in an age where video and photos are so easily accessible and gives people the illusion of possessing power it can be used to alter perceptions of moments and encourage others to blindly believe what they are presenting as photos are ‘factual’ in the sense that they are tangible and can replace reality. For me, taking photographs has always been to capture important moments in my life or in the lives of those around me. Things like birthdays, trips, and celebrations were the only reason I was allowed to use film as they were only to be used sparingly. However, now with how accessible digital media is I am able to capture everything and anything that provokes some form of thought or emotion. In the world we live in, Sontangs emphasis on how photography can be damaging and alter the way in which we capture the world and how we influence others is extremely relevant. Photography combined with image making allows me to present what I want and how I want others to perceive it. Using tools like photoshop allows me to input things like text to add to what it I already have. Image editing tools allow me to create something from scratch. When creating images, I’d like to utilize these tools like photoshop to create images that do not exist in reality but in my head. I am very interested in surreal photography, which is a combination of both photography and image making. Artists like Hieronymus Bosch, whose surrealist art depicts elements of reality and absurdity, are pieces of art that I am inspired by and would like to depict the current state of the world in.

Reading response #003: Graphic Novel:Tetteh's story

Tetteh’s story reflects a traumatic experience that forced her to resort to self defense in return for her life. Illustration allowed for sensitive themes to be portrayed without having the focus be on the violence but rather the emotional distress she endured and continues to endure while in prison. The illustrations vary drastically in that they are drawn using limited colors and are sort of have this look of being traced over photographs giving it a realistic look but while also remaining illustrative. The drawings look similar to rotoscoping as there are different shadows and hues in the drawings which when compared to rotoscoping look similar. The illustrations are all depictions of her memory and which when compared to the photographs are very different because the photograph is of a smiling social worker while the illustrations only depict her in pain. Illustration allows for more freedom when depicting these kinds of stories because it allows for more freedom with adjusting things like expressions, clothing, and by making changes more impactful. I noticed that throughout the story the illustrations would be done in a way that emphasized the illustration that proceeds it. For instance, with the shots of her husband they are composed the same way but the second shot changes the colors and the emotion on the man’s face. It is done to replicate the fear and emotion teethe felt in those particular moments. Another example of this is to show the progression of time after her incarceration. The first shot of her sitting on the bed behind the metal bars feels cold and lonely, followed by the next shot in the exact same composition is different because it depicts her as going through a period of change in time by showing her wearing a different dress but in the same position. The use of illustration is most effective in this story because you are not limited to as many factors as you would be in photography or video. The use of video in the story is also important because it brought another layer to the story by showing the people who advocate for women like Tetteh and showing the reality of the work that is being done to change the outcome for women in these circumstances. The use of illustration and text is integral to these stories because it reads like a comic book which might make it more appealing to wider audiences. The text is also beneficial to her story because they used white font with a black box to detail the events. I believe this was done so as not to distract from the images that are shown but to match the overall tone of Tetteh's story which includes themes of violence against her and her child. Every artistic decision in the story was done to match the overall tone of the story and to best communicate the message by using illustration. Text only media encourages the reader to have a wider interpretation of many details allowing them to have a wide array of ideas while illustrations allow viewers to see exactly, in this case, what occurred and what emotions were felt.
